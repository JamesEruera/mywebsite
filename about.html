About the Developer

Heemi (James) Eruera

Tohunga tārai waka · Navigator · Cultural practitioner

Heemi (James) Eruera is a Māori waka carver, navigator and cultural practitioner of Te Rarawa descent. Raised in Kaitaia, his work is dedicated to the revitalisation and intergenerational transmission of mātauranga Māori, particularly the ancestral practice of tārai waka.

A student of renowned waka builder and navigator Sir Hekenukumai "Hec" Busby, Heemi has developed extensive knowledge of the complete waka-building process, from selecting and shaping the tree through to launching, sailing and navigating the finished vessel. His practice recognises waka as living expressions of whakapapa and as powerful cultural and educational platforms connecting people with one another, the environment and a shared oceanic heritage.

Heemi has led and contributed to significant waka projects, cultural exchanges and learning initiatives throughout Aotearoa and internationally. His collaborations have brought together practitioners and communities from Hawai'i, Tahiti, Taiwan, North America and Europe, creating opportunities to exchange knowledge, strengthen Indigenous relationships and support the continuity of ocean-going traditions. His work includes the Rata waka-carving symposium, international waka exchanges and Tuia te Here Tangata, a contemporary Māori waka created for the Smithsonian Institution.

Through his cultural leadership, mentorship and business networks, Heemi helps build the relationships and resources needed to strengthen waka practice and create meaningful pathways for future generations of practitioners. His hands-on wānanga and collaborative projects bring ancestral knowledge into contemporary settings, advancing tārai waka as a living and evolving artform.
